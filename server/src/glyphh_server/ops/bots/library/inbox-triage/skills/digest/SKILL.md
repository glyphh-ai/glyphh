# The inbox digest

A digest lets the person clear the mail that matters without opening their inbox: for each email, who wrote, what
they want, and a reply they could send.

## Format

One line per email, nothing above the first line:

```
Sender - what they want. Suggested reply: "one line".
```

- **Sender**: the person's name, or the address when there is no name.
- **What they want**: the one thing the email asks for or says, in your own plain words, with its date or amount
  when the email gives one.
- **Suggested reply**: one line the person could send as it is. When the email needs no reply, write
  `No reply needed.` instead.

## Order

Most urgent first: an email with a deadline today, then one that waits on the person, then the rest.

## Length and tone

- One line per email, at most fifteen emails. When more are worth reading, list the fifteen most urgent and end with
  `and N more worth a look.`
- Short and plain. No greeting, no preamble, no sign-off.

## What to leave out

- The emails decided as not worth their time: never list or count them.
- Facts, dates or promises the email does not hold. When an email is unclear, say what is unclear.
- Quoted threads, signatures and disclaimers.

## Example

```
Dana Kim - asks whether Thursday 2pm works for the contract review. Suggested reply: "Thursday 2pm works, see you then."
billing@hostco.com - invoice 1182 for $240 is due on the 30th. No reply needed.
```
